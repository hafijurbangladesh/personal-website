## Context

Digital government services depend on accurate, shared information about the government itself: its offices, organisational units, posts and officials. When every system keeps its own copy of this structure, the copies drift apart and integration becomes harder. A whole-of-government organizational platform gives systems one common foundation for organisational data and access.

Separately, Digital Public Goods (DPG) are open, reusable digital solutions that other countries and institutions can adopt. Preparing national platforms for DPG recognition means looking closely at their openness, documentation and reusability.

## My Role

As Solutions Architect at a2i, I was **Architecture Lead for Doptor**, as well as for **UBID** and **NISE**. I also helped advance **ekShop**, **NISE**, **GRS** and **Doptor** toward Digital Public Goods recognition.

## What was built

Doptor was designed as a shared, whole-of-government platform: a reusable building block that other government systems can rely on rather than duplicate. Architecture leadership meant defining how the platform is structured and how it connects with the wider ecosystem of government services.

The DPG work looked beyond individual systems, positioning several national platforms (ekShop, NISE, GRS and Doptor) as reusable, interoperable solutions with value beyond a single deployment.

## Outcome

This work reflects a DPI mindset: shared foundations and reusable building blocks instead of siloed systems, and national platforms prepared for wider adoption as Digital Public Goods.
