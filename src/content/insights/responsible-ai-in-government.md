Interest in AI across government is high, and with good reason. AI can help officials analyse documents, find information faster and understand how policies are performing. But public institutions work under different expectations than private companies. Decisions must be explainable, data must be protected and services must remain fair to every citizen.

Responsible AI in government is therefore not mainly a question of choosing the right model. It is a question of building the right institutions around it.

## Build institutional capability

AI projects succeed when public servants understand what the technology can and cannot do. Building that understanding, through training, shared guidance and hands-on exposure, is as important as any individual tool. Capability inside institutions also makes it possible to procure, evaluate and oversee AI solutions with confidence.

## Experiment safely

Sandboxes and prototypes let teams test ideas on real problems without putting citizens or sensitive data at risk. A good prototype answers a concrete question: *Is this useful? Is it accurate enough? What safeguards does it need?* It should be cheap to stop if the answer is no.

## Govern from the start

Governance should not be added after deployment. From the first prototype, teams can ask:

- What data is used, and is its use lawful and proportionate?
- Who is accountable for outputs and decisions?
- How will errors and bias be detected and corrected?
- Can the result be explained to the people it affects?

## Connect AI to public infrastructure

Finally, AI in government works best on top of solid digital foundations: interoperable systems, good data and shared platforms. Responsible AI and Digital Public Infrastructure are two parts of the same agenda: digital government that is trustworthy, inclusive and built to last.
